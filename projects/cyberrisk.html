01Problem

A bank, insurer or lender with exposure to an industrial asset has no good way to turn "the refinery's control network was compromised" into a number. The security tooling speaks in severities and CVEs; the credit committee speaks in currency. Nothing bridges the two while it still matters.

This is a cyber incident risk engine with a financial layer on top — explicitly not an investment-risk tool. It answers what a given incident on a given asset is likely to cost, and flags the credit consequence.

02Design

Why a graph and not a function

Each stage is an agent with one job and an explicit contract with the next: detector, classifier, isolator, quantifier, reporter. Staging it as a DAG rather than one procedure means a stage can be swapped, re-run or inspected alone, and the state at every hop is a readable object rather than a local variable buried in a call stack. When an incident report is later disputed, the chain that produced it can be walked backwards.

Isolation is an action, not a recommendation

The isolator does not file a ticket. It publishes micro-segmentation commands over MQTT and cuts the device off. Containment that waits on a human has already lost the time that mattered.

Components stay separate

Downtime, SLA and regulatory exposure are reported individually rather than rolled into one headline figure, because they carry very different confidence. Downtime cost is an estimate of an estimate; a regulatory band is closer to a known quantity. A reader needs to be able to discount them independently — which is why the console shows all four lines rather than just the total.

04Limits
Where the numbers come from

The financial model is only as good as its per-device cost parameters, which are entered, not learned. Hourly downtime cost, SLA terms and regulatory regime are inputs supplied by whoever configures the asset. The engine does arithmetic on them faithfully and has no way to know when they are optimistic.

That is also the honest reading of the console above: every figure it produces is a consequence of sliders you set. Treating such an output as a forecast rather than a parameterised estimate is the failure mode this class of tool invites, and the separation of components is the main defence against it.
