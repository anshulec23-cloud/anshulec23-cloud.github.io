01Problem

Supervised anomaly detection in video has a dataset problem no amount of architecture fixes: anomalies are by definition the thing you have fewest examples of. Collecting a labelled corpus of fights and break-ins for every new camera angle is not a plan.

So Sentinel inverts it. Train only on normal movement and treat reconstruction failure as the signal. The model never needs to see an anomaly to flag one.

02Design

Identity is what makes motion meaningful

A bounding box alone says nothing about behaviour. DeepSORT holds a person's identity across frames, which turns a sequence of boxes into a trajectory — and trajectories are where loitering, sprinting and scuffles actually live. Lose the track ID and the anomaly disappears with it.

Nine numbers per person per frame

Each track carries a compact feature vector: normalised centroid, normalised box dimensions, velocity in x and y, scalar speed, acceleration, and clamped aspect ratio. Everything is normalised against frame dimensions and the frame diagonal, so the model is not relearning the scene every time the camera resolution or position changes.

Aspect ratio is the quiet one. A standing person and a person on the ground differ sharply in it, which is what separates a fall or a tackle from fast walking.

Model plus rules, not model alone

The detector fuses autoencoder reconstruction error with explicit rules. The model catches the unknown category — movement unlike anything in training — while the rules keep named classes stable and interpretable.

Turn fusion off in the console and scrub to frame 150. There is a blip there that crosses the threshold and is not an event. With fusion on it stays quiet; with fusion off it fires. That is the trade the switch makes visible: fusion costs you sensitivity to genuinely novel behaviour in exchange for far fewer false alarms, and which side you want depends on whether a human reviews every alert.

04Limits

Unsupervised detection trades a labelling problem for a threshold problem. Reconstruction error has no natural cut-off: set it tight and a crowded platform generates noise, set it loose and genuine events slip past. The rule layer covers part of that, and exists partly because an operator will not trust a number they cannot interrogate.

Inherited weakness

Accuracy inherits YOLOv8's. Heavy occlusion, unusual camera angles and crowds degrade detection, and anything the detector misses the tracker never sees — errors compound down the pipeline rather than cancelling out.
