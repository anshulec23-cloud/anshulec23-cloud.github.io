Image Forgery Detector

Screening financial and legal documents for manipulation by reading the compression history of an image rather than its content — and showing an analyst exactly which region drove the verdict.

Domain
Image forensics
Classes
Real / tampered / AI-generated
Stack
PyTorch, ResNet18, Streamlit
Licence
Apache-2.0

The problem

A forged payslip, invoice or certificate is usually not a crude cut-and-paste any more. It is a competent edit, exported once, and it looks correct to a human reviewer with forty of them to clear before lunch.

The giveaway is rarely visible in the picture. It is in the compression history: a region that has been through a different number of JPEG generations than its surroundings carries a different error signature, whatever it looks like to the eye.

Two streams, because one is not enough

Error Level Analysis alone is brittle — it reacts to any recompression, including innocent ones, and a detector built on it flags half the internet. Raw RGB alone misses the forensic signal entirely and learns content instead of provenance.

Running both through separate ResNet18 backbones and concatenating the 512-dimensional features into a single fusion head lets the classifier weigh them against each other: unusual compression residue in a region whose content also looks off is a far stronger signal than either on its own.

Three classes, not two

Splitting ai_generated out from tampered matters because the two failure modes are different problems. A spliced region in a real photo and a wholly synthesised image have almost nothing in common forensically, and collapsing them into one "fake" class forces the model to learn an incoherent boundary.

Training it in two phases

Epochs 1–3 train the classification head alone with both backbones frozen, so the randomly initialised head does not blow gradients back through pretrained features. From epoch 4 the whole network fine-tunes at the configured learning rate. The best validation checkpoint is kept automatically.

Explanation is part of the output

Grad-CAM hooks the final convolutional layer and produces a heat map over the suspect region. For a screening tool this is not decoration — an analyst cannot act on a bare probability, and a highlighted region is what makes the verdict checkable by a human who disagrees with it.

Honest limits

Read this before judging the numbers

The repository ships the pipeline and the training loop, not trained weights. The demo weights are randomly initialised: the application runs end to end, but its predictions are meaningless until you train on a real corpus. Any accuracy claim would have to come from your own run.

ELA is also a dated signal. It is strong against conventional splicing and retouching, and weaker against modern generative models that never produce an intermediate JPEG at all — which is exactly why the raw RGB stream carries the ai_generated class rather than ELA doing all the work.

Recommended public datasets for training are listed in the README: CASIA v2.0 and RAISE for real and tampered, ArtiFact and CIFAKE for generated.
