01Problem

A forged payslip, invoice or certificate is rarely a crude cut-and-paste any more. It is a competent edit, exported once, and it looks correct to a reviewer with forty of them to clear before lunch.

The giveaway is usually not visible in the picture. It is in the compression history: a region that has been through a different number of JPEG generations than its surroundings carries a different error signature, whatever it looks like to the eye. That is precisely what the console above exposes — the numbers in the body of the invoice have been compressed twice, the altered total only once.

02Design

Two streams, because one is not enough

Error level analysis alone is brittle. It reacts to any recompression, including innocent ones, and a detector built on it flags half the internet — drag the quality slider down in the console and watch the whole page light up. Raw RGB alone misses the forensic signal entirely and learns content instead of provenance.

Running both through separate ResNet18 backbones and concatenating the 512-dimensional features into one fusion head lets the classifier weigh them against each other: unusual compression residue in a region whose content also looks off is a far stronger signal than either alone.

Three classes, not two

Splitting ai_generated out from tampered matters because the failure modes are different problems. A spliced region in a real photograph and a wholly synthesised image have almost nothing in common forensically, and collapsing them into one "fake" class forces the model to learn an incoherent boundary.

Two-phase training

Epochs 1–3 train the classification head alone with both backbones frozen, so a randomly initialised head does not blow gradients back through pretrained features. From epoch 4 the whole network fine-tunes at the configured rate. The best validation checkpoint is kept automatically.

Explanation is part of the output

Grad-CAM hooks the final convolutional layer and produces a heat map over the suspect region. For a screening tool this is not decoration — an analyst cannot act on a bare probability, and a highlighted region is what makes a verdict checkable by someone who disagrees with it.

04Limits
Read this before judging the numbers

The repository ships the pipeline and the training loop, not trained weights. The demo weights are randomly initialised: the application runs end to end, but its predictions are meaningless until it is trained on a real corpus. Any accuracy figure would have to come from your own run.

ELA is also a dated signal. It is strong against conventional splicing and retouching and weak against modern generative models, which never produce an intermediate JPEG at all — which is exactly why the raw-pixel stream carries the ai_generated class rather than ELA doing all the work.

Recommended public datasets are listed in the README: CASIA v2.0 and RAISE for real and tampered, ArtiFact and CIFAKE for generated.
